Cultivate consistent Bible reading — drift by drift

Drift by Drift · October 4, 2026 · Psalm 19:7–8 · Dan Allen · CrossWay Milwaukee

Scripture — Psalm 19:7–8 (ESV)

7 The law of the Lord is perfect, reviving the soul; the testimony of the Lord is sure, making wise the simple; 8 the precepts of the Lord are right, rejoicing the heart; the commandment of the Lord is pure, enlightening the eyes.

Context

  • Once a year CrossWay focuses a sermon on cultivating consistent Bible reading. The series is called Drift by Drift — the conviction that God grows people through his Word not by a one-week blast, but year after year, like a tree planted by streams of water (Psalm 1).
  • This message is part 5 and leans heavily into application: tips for motivation, mindset, and methods. Earlier parts are available on the church website; a few more tips are planned for future years.
  • Brought earlier than usual this year (October 4, 2026); next year it will likely return later on the calendar.

Theme summary

  • Keystone habit: Like Charles Duhigg’s keystone habits (or a keystone species in an ecosystem), one well-placed practice can set off a chain of good fruit — improved sleep is an everyday example; regular Scripture engagement is the spiritual parallel.
  • Power of Four: An eight-year study with over 100,000 respondents worldwide found that engaging Scripture at least four times a week is linked to dramatic positive change — far stronger than reading one to three times a week (which looks closer to not reading at all), and even more powerful than church services and activities alone.
  • God’s promises: Psalm 19:7–8 names what the Word does — revive the soul, make wise the simple, rejoice the heart, enlighten the eyes. Add Psalm 119:11 (stored Word guards from sin), Romans 15:4 (Scripture gives hope), and 1 Thessalonians 2:13 (the Word works in those who believe).
  • Gospel clarity first: How often you read does not determine your standing with God. Right standing is through Christ’s death, burial, and resurrection alone. After trust in Christ, God gives a new heart and the Spirit — and with that, a thirst for the Word (sometimes strong, sometimes small).

Main points

Focus energy on the keystone: consistent Bible reading

If God’s Word produces this kind of chain reaction in believers, it deserves focused energy — not as legalism, but as wise attention to where the greatest impact comes from.

  • The Power of Four findings (as presented): proactive faith up dramatically (reported ~218–416%); personal struggles down (~14–60%); risky behaviors down (~20–69%) when people engage Scripture four or more times a week.
  • That should not surprise us — it tracks with what God already promised his Word will do in Psalm 19 and elsewhere.
  • Tips fall into three buckets: motivation (stir the heart by grace, not legalism), mindset (right expectations), and methods (on-the-ground tools). Today hits about six — one motivation, one mindset, and several methods.

1. Motivation — the chain reaction

Remind your heart of what God’s Word actually does. Pick a benefit God promises and let that fuel desire — grace-motivated, not performance-driven.

  • Revive soul, wisdom, rejoicing heart, enlightened eyes, hope, protection from sin, inward change that bears fruit — these are not marketing claims; they are God’s promises.
  • CrossWay wants to be a church that keeps encouraging one another toward the Word: alone and together, drift by drift.

2. Mindset — expect “Huh?”

Limited creatures will often open Scripture and not fully understand. That is normal — not a reason to quit — and it can deepen fellowship with God.

  • Intellectual barriers: Passages like Galatians 4:22–26 (Hagar / Sinai / two covenants) or the hard numbers at the end of Daniel can leave even careful readers saying, “What is going on?”
  • Emotional barriers: The end of Judges, “take up your cross,” or rejoicing in weakness can unsettle us — “What do I do with this?”
  • The problem is not with the Scriptures; it is with us. The doctrine of the perspicuity (clarity) of Scripture means the main message is clear enough even for a child (cf. Deuteronomy 6; 2 Timothy 3:14–15) — yet some parts remain hard. Even Peter said Paul writes things that are hard to understand (2 Peter 3:15–16).
  • “Huh?” can invite deeper fellowship: keep reading and worshiping even when you do not yet understand. It also reorients us — we need a God we cannot shrink to the size of our brains (Mike Moore).
  • Do not use confusion as an excuse to stop. The principle says: keep going; seek to know this God more.

3. Method — Question Power-Up

A simple daily question can stir follow-through: “Will you read God’s word today?”

  • Researchers call this the question-behavior effect (studied for decades since around 1980): asking yourself whether you will do a valued behavior tends to increase follow-through.
  • Possible reasons: cognitive dissonance (discomfort with “no,” or obligation after “yes”); decisive ownership (“will you,” not “can you / would you / can we”); and visualization of the action and its good.
  • Practical delivery: recurring email (e.g. Zapier), a friend’s text, a paper note — and answer it in writing or by checking a box, not only in your head.

4. Method — best time (available + alert)

As limited people, some hours are more productive than others. Aim to read when you are both available and alert — a kind of firstfruits offering of your best attention.

  • Available: few competing distractions. Alert: able to sustain focus for that stretch of time. You need both; after work you may be available but foggy; early morning you may be alert but the house is not available.
  • Scripture does not mandate a clock time. Psalm 1 speaks of meditating day and night — morning is not holier than evening. Best is when you are most alert and available.
  • Firstfruits picture: offering God not leftovers, but a prime portion of the day in worshipful response.

5. Method — unique seasons & special dates

Calendars have stretches that do not match the usual rhythm. Plan time and material so those seasons boost Bible reading instead of sidelining it.

  • Unique seasons: new baby, holiday guests, a crushing work project, vacation with a flexible schedule. Decide ahead when you will read and what you will read (e.g. switch from Deuteronomy to Jonah for a four-day trip; or lean into Genesis when you have downtime and need your mind filled well).
  • Special dates: use daylight saving’s extra hour, a birthday Psalm matching your age, anniversary, Palm Sunday’s Passion week, New Year’s, Christmas — intentional boosts rather than letting the habit get crowded out.

6. Method — the DOG prayer

God invites his people to call for help. Ask him for Discipline, Open ears, and Gladness as you come to the Word.

  • D — Discipline: “Lord, give me discipline to keep this habit; help me against laziness and tiredness.”
  • O — Open ears: Echo Psalm 119 — “Open my eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of your law”; teach me; give understanding and humility to receive your Word over my opinions.
  • G — Gladness: Ask him to turn your heart toward his testimonies and fill you with joy in his presence (cf. Psalm 16:11).
  • Picture: “Dogs have masters; cats have staff.” There is an independent “cat” in us — and, in those who know Jesus, also a “dog” that wants to live for the Master’s joy. The DOG prayer leans into that.
  • Why we are heard: not because of our Bible reading streak, but because of Christ’s death, burial, and resurrection. We come to the Table on that same gospel footing.

Key idea

Regular engagement with God’s Word is a keystone habit that shapes a life — by grace, not to earn standing with God. Expect “Huh?”, use simple tools, and ask him for discipline, open ears, and gladness as he grows you drift by drift.

Discussion questions

1

Where have you treated Bible reading more like a guilt meter than a keystone habit of grace? How does the gospel clarity in this sermon — standing with God by Christ’s work alone — free you to cultivate thirst without legalism?

2

When you hit a “Huh?” moment in Scripture (intellectual or emotional), what do you usually do — quit, skim, or lean in? Which part of the mindset teaching (clarity of Scripture, Peter’s honesty, fellowship, a God bigger than your brain) do you most need this week?

3

Which one tip will you actually try: chain-reaction reminders, the daily question “Will you read God’s word today?”, finding your available-and-alert slot, planning for a unique season or special date, or the DOG prayer? What is your concrete next step?

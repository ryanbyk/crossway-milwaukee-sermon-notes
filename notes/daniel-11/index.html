Small Group Discussion Notes

Stand firm — God refines through suffering

Daniel 11 · Dan Allen · CrossWay Milwaukee

Context

  • Daniel 10–12 is one vision. Chapter 10 showed that God controls suffering; chapter 11 presses further — there is design in it. God purifies and refines his people through the chaos of history.
  • This is apocalyptic literature. Reading tip for the week: keep the focal point — tap your phone camera on God and his people so the swirling kings and wars stay in the background, still true, but oriented around that focus.
  • The opening image is a “shaggy dog story”: a long winding setup with no real punch line. The sermon asks whether history — and your hardships — are that empty, or whether God has purpose in the pain.

Passage summary

  • Persia to Greece (vv. 2–4): Selective flyover of ~200 years — Persia falls to Greece; Alexander’s empire splits into four weaker kingdoms. The rest of the chapter zooms in on two of those dynasties: North and South.
  • North vs South, Israel in the middle (vv. 5–20): Dynasties war for centuries. God’s people (back in the land) sit between them — like Milwaukee between Green Bay and Chicago. Armies “pass through”; later the northern king stands in the glorious land “with destruction in his hand.”
  • Antiochus Epiphanes and worse (vv. 21–45): A contemptible northern king rages against the holy covenant — temple desecrated, offerings stopped, abomination set up. The vision then pans out toward the end: evil intensifies, God’s people still suffer, yet the tyrant “shall come to his end with none to help.”
  • How they stand (vv. 32b–35): “The people who know their God shall stand firm and take action.” Some are beaten down “so that they may be refined, purified, and made white” until the appointed time.

Main points

This is not a shaggy dog story

History’s long winding suffering is not anticlimax with no punch line. God wants Daniel’s readers — and us — to know there is design in the pain his people endure.

  • Controlling suffering (ch. 10) is not enough; a comedian can “control” a pointless story. We need to know God purposes refining through it.
  • Following God does not prevent suffering — in this vision it guarantees it, and things go from bad to worse before the end.
  • Wrong responses already appear in the text: violent revolt that “shall fail” (v. 14), and those seduced by flattery who violate the covenant (v. 32a).

Keep God’s people as the focal point

Daniel 11 moves fast across centuries. If the kings become the subject, you miss the point — how God’s people experience the chaos, and how some stand while others fall.

  • “King of the North / South” names dynasties across generations, not one man. Details matter, but only as they relate to Jerusalem and the covenant people.
  • Israel is the corridor of the conflict. Passing armies, occupation, and destruction land on real families — not merely policy changes.
  • Antiochus (and the still-worse end-time figure) prospers in defiance of God for a time — which makes the call to stand even sharper.

Those who know their God stand firm

Verse 32 is the key: power to endure comes from knowing God personally — not merely knowing about him, books, or services.

  • What do they know? Verse 35’s purpose clause: some of the wise are beaten down so that they may be refined, purified, and made white. God designs suffering to strip impurities and shape people toward Christ.
  • Illustration: enduring a painful treatment because you trust the practitioner knows what he’s doing. Knowing God’s design gives strength to stay under the hand that hurts in order to heal.
  • Same thread elsewhere: Psalm 119:67; Romans 5:3–5; 1 Peter 1:6–7; 2 Corinthians 12:7–10 — suffering produces endurance, character, hope; faith refined like gold; grace sufficient in weakness.

The cross is the clearest proof

The darkest day in history was also the brightest — God used the suffering of the Son for the good of his people and the glory of his name.

  • This promise is for those who know Christ. Apart from him, the worst days are still ahead; in him, the Father cares for you as a perfect Father through hardship.
  • Safety and comfort are not guaranteed. Lament remains honest. But the path is refining, not pointless.
  • At the Table: bring present thorns, fears, and losses, and recommit them to the Lord who refines through suffering.

Key idea

Your hardships are not a shaggy dog story. God not only controls suffering — he designs it to refine those who know him, so they can stand firm until the end.

Discussion questions

1

Where have you been tempted to treat your pain like a “shaggy dog story” — long, hard, and ultimately pointless? What changes if you take seriously that God designs refining through it?

2

Daniel 11 shows two failed responses: grabbing control by force, and caving to flattery to avoid suffering. Which of those pulls harder on you right now — and what would “knowing your God” and standing firm look like instead?

3

Romans 5 and 1 Peter 1 say suffering can grow endurance, character, hope, and proven faith. Looking back, where has hardship already refined you — and what present struggle do you need to offer back to God at the Table this week?
